# Pecan Dukkah

## Details

This is a take on Egyptian dukkah with a Southern twist. Sprinkle it over deviled eggs, flatbread, salads, hummus, salmon, and even cream cheese bagels.

## Ingredients

- 2 tsp fennel seeds
- 1/4 tsp cayenne
- 1 star anise
- 1/2 Cup raw pecans
- 1 tsp ground cumin
- 1/4 Cup sesame seeds
- 1 tsp ground coriander
- Salt
- 1 tsp light brown sugar

## Steps

1. Grind the spices, then the pecans. Stir in the sesame and salt.

## Full

Grind the spices: Using a mortar and pestle, grind the fennel seeds, star anise, cumin, coriander, brown sugar, and cayenne until no large pieces of spice remain.

Add in the pecans and continue grinding until they break down into small chunks, then add the sesame seeds and season with salt to taste.

Tip: Breaking everything up in a mortar and pestle is the way to go for dukkah (which literally translates to "to crush" or "to pound" in Arabic). It releases the oils from the spices and nuts and marries all the notes. A spice grinder, or toasting the nuts, can make the flavors too overpowering. Store this blend in an airtight container in the cabinet for up to 1 month.

## Original

**From:** We Fancy

**Makes about 3/4 cup**  
**(EF, GF, RSF, S30)**

This is my take on the Egyptian spice dukkah, but with a Southern twist. You'll probably want to sprinkle this over everything, like I do: Sky-High Avocado Deviled Eggs (page 55), flatbread (see page 92), salads, hummus, salmon filets, and even cream cheese bagels.

## Ingredients

- fennel seeds, 2 teaspoons
- ground cayenne, 1/4 teaspoon
- star anise, 1 whole
- raw pecan pieces or halves, 1/2 cup
- ground cumin, 1 teaspoon
- sesame seeds, 1/4 cup
- ground coriander, 1 teaspoon
- kosher salt
- light brown sugar, 1 teaspoon

## Instructions

**Grind the spices:** Using a mortar and pestle (see Tip), grind the fennel seeds, star anise, cumin, coriander, brown sugar, and cayenne until no large pieces of spice remain.

**Add in the pecans** and continue grinding until they break down into small chunks, then add the sesame seeds and season with salt to taste.

## Tip

Breaking everything up in a mortar and pestle is the way to go for dukkah (which literally translates to "to crush" or "to pound" in Arabic). It releases the oils from the spices and nuts and marries all the notes. I prefer this method to a spice grinder, or toasting the nuts, which can make the flavors too overpowering. Store this blend in an airtight container in the cabinet for up to 1 month.
