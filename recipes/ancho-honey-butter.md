# Ancho Honey Butter

## Details

Honey butter with the warmth and fruitiness of mild Mexican chiles plus the aroma of oranges. It is perfect for cornbread and cornbread waffles. Slather it on corn cobs, dollop it in a baked sweet potato, swirl it into refried beans, or baste it over roasted Brussels sprouts.

Make it fancy: Add a splash of pure vanilla extract to the butter before blending. It enhances the smokiness of the ancho chiles.

## Ingredients

- 2 dried ancho chiles
- 2 sticks room-temperature unsalted butter
- 3 Tbs honey
- Zest of 1 orange
- 1/4 tsp salt

## Optional

- Splash of vanilla

## Steps

1. Toast the chiles at 375°F for 3–4 minutes. Soak 15–20 minutes, then blend into the butter.

## Full

Toast the chiles: Preheat the oven to 375°F. Place the chiles on a small sheet pan and bake for 3 to 4 minutes, until fragrant. Remove from the oven and let them cool slightly before removing the stems and discarding the seeds.

Soak the chiles: In a medium bowl, cover the chiles with enough water to submerge. Allow the chiles to soak for 15 to 20 minutes, until softened and cooled slightly. Drain the water.

Blend the butter: In the bowl of a mini food processor, blend the butter, drained chiles, honey, orange zest, and salt on high into a smooth puree.

Store: In the center of a sheet of parchment paper, place the butter and roll it into a tight log, twisting the ends closed. Refrigerate for up to 1 month until you're ready to use, or freeze for up to 6 months.

Tip: Ancho chiles don't pack nearly as much heat as chipotle peppers or chiles de arbol, so this is a mild chile, and the one usually found in commercial ground chili powders.

## Ingredients

- dried ancho chiles, 2 whole (about 1 to 1.5 ounces)
- unsalted butter, 2 sticks, at room temperature
- honey, 3 tablespoons
- orange zest from 1 orange
- kosher salt, 1/4 teaspoon

## Make It Fancy

Add a splash of pure vanilla extract to the butter before blending, which surprisingly enhances the smokiness of the ancho chiles because it also has warm, smoky undertones.

## Instructions

**Toast the chiles:** Preheat the oven to 375°F. Place the chiles on a small sheet pan and bake for 3 to 4 minutes, until fragrant. Remove from the oven and let them cool slightly before removing the stems and discarding the seeds.

**Soak the chiles:** In a medium bowl, cover the chiles with enough water to submerge. Allow the chiles to soak for 15 to 20 minutes, until softened and cooled slightly. Drain the water.

**Blend the butter:** In the bowl of a mini food processor, blend the butter, drained chiles, honey, orange zest, and salt on high into a smooth puree.

**Store:** In the center of a sheet of parchment paper, place the butter and roll it into a tight log, twisting the ends closed. Refrigerate for up to 1 month until you're ready to use, or freeze for up to 6 months.

## Tip

Ancho chiles don't pack nearly as much heat as chipotle peppers or chiles de arbol, so it's a good neutral chile, and the one you'd usually find in the commercial ground chili powders at the store.
